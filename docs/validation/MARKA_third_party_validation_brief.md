# MARKA

## Think on paper. Perform under pressure.

### Third-Party Concept Validation Brief

**Initial audience:** Graduate applicants preparing for general aptitude assessments
**Document status:** Discussion draft for validation—not a claim of proven outcomes
**Date:** 5 September 2026

> MARKA is a hybrid self-evaluation platform that teaches repeatable paper-working methods for aptitude problems, then tests whether those methods produce faster, accurate performance in timed CBT simulations.

This brief is intended for conversations with graduate applicants, career coaches, training providers, university career centres, and assessment professionals. It presents the product hypothesis, the proposed experience, the business model, and the questions that still require evidence.

---

## 1. Executive snapshot

### The problem we believe exists

Many graduate applicants can solve numerical and reasoning problems when practising casually, but lose accuracy or fail to finish when time pressure is introduced. Most preparation products provide more questions, mock tests, and answer explanations. They rarely teach a repeatable physical method for organising calculations and decisions quickly on paper.

### The proposed solution

MARKA teaches structured paper-working techniques, provides a curated question depot, captures paper-practice answers through a reusable hand-drawn answer sheet, and validates progress using timed CBT simulations.

### The central hypothesis

If applicants learn and repeatedly practise efficient paper-working methods, they will reduce their time per correct answer without sacrificing accuracy—and that improvement will transfer into CBT performance.

### What must be validated

- Is the speed-and-accuracy problem painful enough to motivate payment?
- Will applicants repeatedly use a reusable answer sheet?
- Do the taught methods create measurable improvement?
- Does improvement achieved during paper practice transfer to CBT?
- Is the complete experience meaningfully better than practising only with online quizzes?

## 2. The product in one sentence

**For graduate applicants who understand aptitude questions but lose speed or accuracy under pressure, MARKA teaches repeatable paper-working methods and validates them in timed CBT simulations—so learners can improve how they reason, not merely complete more questions.**

### Brand promise

**Think on paper. Perform under pressure.**

### What MARKA is not

- It is not a collection of leaked or purportedly official employer questions.
- It does not claim affiliation with employers or assessment providers.
- It is not another undifferentiated question-and-timer application.
- It does not guarantee employment or a particular assessment score.
- It does not initially accept arbitrary user-uploaded PDFs.
- It does not claim that paper is inherently superior for every learner or every task.

---

## 3. Who MARKA serves

### Initial customer

A graduate applicant preparing for general numerical, verbal, logical, abstract, critical-reasoning, data-interpretation, or situational-judgement assessments.

This user commonly has one or more of the following behaviours:

- Practises from PDFs, downloaded packs, books, or shared documents.
- Uses paper for calculations even when questions appear on a screen.
- Understands many questions but works too slowly under a timer.
- Repeats generic mock tests without knowing which working habit is inefficient.
- Wants evidence of improvement before an important assessment.

### Initial job to be done

> “Help me develop a faster, more reliable way to work through aptitude questions and prove that I can still perform when the test moves to a timed computer screen.”

### Future users

If the model works for graduate aptitude preparation, the same system may later support professional certifications, scholarship tests, entrance examinations, academic self-evaluation, and other time-constrained objective assessments. These are expansion hypotheses, not first-release scope.

## 4. The MARKA learning loop

1. **Diagnose:** Complete a short baseline CBT assessment.
2. **Learn:** Study one efficient paper-working method.
3. **Practise:** Apply the method to a focused paper question set.
4. **Capture:** Record answers on a reusable hand-drawn MARKA sheet and scan it.
5. **Review:** See accuracy, mistakes, intended methods, and faster solution paths.
6. **Apply pressure:** Complete a parallel timed CBT checkpoint.
7. **Compare:** Measure the change in speed, accuracy, and paper-to-CBT transfer.
8. **Continue:** Receive the next recommended method or drill.

The reusable answer sheet is drawn once. When cleanly erased, it can support repeated practice sessions. The sheet is an input tool; the enduring value is the training curriculum and performance history around it.

---

## 5. What MARKA teaches

MARKA would develop a library of named, repeatable working techniques: the **MARKA Method**. Each technique must be simple enough to remember, fast enough to use under pressure, and testable against an alternative approach.

### Illustrative numerical techniques

- Percentage ladders
- Ratio boxes
- Difference tables
- Approximation before calculation
- Unit-normalisation layouts
- Answer-option elimination
- Three-line data extraction
- Stop-loss rules for time-consuming questions

### Illustrative logical and verbal techniques

- Constraint grids
- Sequence-difference maps
- Possibility elimination
- Claim-and-evidence marking
- Contradiction checks
- Passage-navigation notation

These names are illustrative. The first pilot should develop and test only three numerical techniques before finalising the wider curriculum.

### Training progression

**See the method → copy the method → use it independently → use it against a soft timer → use it under full pressure → prove transfer in CBT.**

## 6. The question depot

The depot is a controlled training environment, not an unverified file repository. First-release questions are original, licensed, openly usable, or otherwise cleared for use. AI may assist with variation generation, but every published question and answer must receive human review.

Every question should be tagged with:

- Skill and subskill
- Intended working method
- Alternative valid method
- Difficulty
- Target completion time
- Common wrong approach
- Distractor logic
- Calculation intensity
- Suitability for paper practice, CBT, or both
- Parallel question family

Parallel families are essential. A learner should practise one item on paper and encounter an equivalent—but not memorised—item in the CBT checkpoint.

### The content standard

Every solution should show:

1. The correct answer
2. Compact paper working
3. Why that method fits the question
4. A slower or riskier route to avoid
5. The common trap
6. A target completion time
7. The next related drill

## 7. Paper practice and CBT are one product

MARKA is one brand and one learning record. Paper Practice and CBT Simulation are two response modes inside the same product.

| Dimension | Conventional online quiz | MARKA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary action | Select answers on screen | Learn on paper; validate on screen |
| Instruction | Correct answer and explanation | Repeatable working method and faster path |
| Practice evidence | Screen attempts | Paper and CBT attempts in one history |
| Timing insight | Total and per-question screen time | Paper session pace, CBT item time, and transfer gap |
| Progression | More questions or higher difficulty | Method → drill → pressure → transfer |
| Core output | Score | Readiness profile and next training action |

### Honest timing boundaries

In Paper Practice, MARKA can measure total session time, questions attempted, accuracy, questions per minute, and time per correct answer. It cannot truthfully know the time spent on each paper question from one final scan.

In CBT Simulation, MARKA can measure item-level response time, skips, revisits, answer changes, pace deterioration, and deadline behaviour. The value comes from comparing these signals with paper-practice performance.

---

## 8. The readiness profile

MARKA should report understandable measurements rather than an unexplained AI score.

- **Method:** Is the learner selecting an efficient approach?
- **Accuracy:** Does the approach produce correct answers?
- **Speed:** How quickly do correct answers arrive?
- **Consistency:** Can the learner repeat the performance?
- **Pressure:** Does performance deteriorate as the deadline approaches?
- **Transfer:** Does paper-practice competence survive in CBT?

A headline readiness indicator may summarise these dimensions, but the underlying measurements must remain visible.

### Example outcome statement

> “Across equivalent percentage questions, your median time per correct answer fell from 94 seconds to 76 seconds while accuracy remained at 84%. Your next constraint is interpreting multi-column tables under time pressure.”

This type of outcome must only be shown when supported by sufficient, comparable attempt data.

---

## 9. Why this may be valuable

MARKA is based on a careful, testable proposition—not the claim that paper is always better. Research on external representations suggests that writing and diagrams can do more than store information; they can influence how a problem is represented and solved. Research also warns that ineffective drawing strategies can reduce performance, which supports teaching specific methods rather than simply telling learners to “use paper.” Studies comparing computer and paper testing have found nuanced results rather than a universal paper advantage, while observing greater scratch-paper use for some algorithmic and difficult tasks.

The product opportunity is therefore to teach good external-working strategies and measure whether they transfer—not to market paper as magic.

## 10. Business-model hypothesis

### Initial offer

A paid seven-day numerical-reasoning programme containing:

- A baseline CBT
- Three signature MARKA methods
- Five focused paper drills
- Reusable-answer-sheet scanning
- Three timed CBT checkpoints
- A final parallel assessment
- A personal speed-and-accuracy report
- Approximately 100–150 human-reviewed questions

### Initial pricing test

Test willingness to pay between **₦2,000 and ₦4,000** for the complete programme. This is a validation range, not a final price.

### Potential later revenue

- Skill-specific programmes
- Monthly or assessment-season access to the depot
- University career-centre and training-provider licences
- Sponsored access for graduate cohorts
- Physical MARKA practice kits or durable reusable sheets

The early business should sell measurable training outcomes, not individual scans.

---

## 11. Defensibility hypothesis

The scanner alone is replicable. Defensibility would need to accumulate across several layers:

1. A recognisable category: paper-working performance training
2. A named and teachable MARKA Method curriculum
3. Method-tagged and calibrated question families
4. Reliable reusable-sheet capture
5. Paper-to-CBT transfer measurement
6. Longitudinal speed-and-accuracy data
7. Demonstrated learner outcomes
8. Trusted distribution through career communities and training partners

The strongest long-term asset would be evidence showing which methods improve speed for which learners and question types.

---

## 12. Key risks and unknowns

- Learners may like the concept but prefer selecting answers directly on screen.
- Paper improvement may not transfer into CBT performance.
- The method curriculum may be difficult to teach consistently.
- Scanning or erasing may introduce enough friction to damage repeat usage.
- Producing and reviewing high-quality parallel questions may be expensive.
- The product may attract users only immediately before an assessment.
- A speed focus may encourage rushing and reduce accuracy.
- Some assessments may restrict scratch-paper use; MARKA must not imply otherwise.
- Outcome claims may become misleading if question difficulty is not calibrated.

These risks are reasons to run a controlled pilot, not assumptions to hide.

## 13. Proposed validation pilot

### Participants

Recruit 30–50 graduate applicants who expect to take aptitude assessments within the next six months. Include a mix of confident and struggling candidates.

### Pilot sequence

1. Run a baseline numerical-reasoning CBT.
2. Teach three methods covering percentages, ratios, and data interpretation.
3. Assign five paper-practice sessions over seven days.
4. Scan each session using the reusable sheet.
5. Run calibrated CBT checkpoints with parallel questions.
6. Conduct a final assessment and interview participants.
7. Ask a meaningful subset to pay before or during the pilot.

### Proposed decision thresholds

- At least 15% improvement in median time per correct answer
- No material decline in accuracy
- At least 60% programme completion
- At least 90% first-attempt scan success
- Evidence of repeated voluntary paper practice
- At least 20 paying participants without individual persuasion
- Qualitative feedback that the working methods—not merely the interface—created value

These thresholds should be agreed before the pilot begins and interpreted alongside sample size and question comparability.

## 14. Questions for third-party validation

### For graduate applicants

1. Tell us about the last aptitude test you prepared for. What was most difficult?
2. What materials did you use, and how did you record your working?
3. When did time pressure begin to affect your decisions?
4. How did you determine which kinds of questions were slowing you down?
5. What did existing practice products fail to teach you?
6. How do you currently check answers completed from a PDF or book?
7. What would make a reusable answer sheet useful or annoying?
8. Would you prefer a seven-day guided programme or unrestricted depot access? Why?
9. What evidence of improvement would you trust?
10. What have you previously paid for aptitude preparation?

Avoid asking, “Would you use MARKA?” Ask about actual past behaviour, spending, and concrete trade-offs.

### For coaches and training providers

1. Which paper-working habits most often separate fast and slow candidates?
2. Can those habits be codified without oversimplifying the reasoning?
3. What learner work would you need to see before diagnosing an inefficient method?
4. How do you currently measure improvement beyond total score?
5. Would paper-to-CBT transfer data change how you coach a learner?
6. What would prevent you from recommending or purchasing this programme?

### For recruiters and assessment professionals

1. Which general reasoning skills create the greatest difficulty for entry-level applicants?
2. How important are speed, accuracy, and question-selection strategy relative to one another?
3. In which assessment settings is scratch paper allowed, restricted, or irrelevant?
4. Which preparation claims would appear credible, and which would appear misleading?
5. What evidence would be required before treating a readiness measure as meaningful?

## 17. The decision this document supports

MARKA is not seeking confirmation that the idea sounds interesting. It is seeking evidence about whether:

1. The problem is urgent and commercially meaningful.
2. The reusable paper workflow is acceptable in repeated use.
3. Specific paper-working methods improve speed without harming accuracy.
4. Those improvements transfer to CBT.
5. Graduate applicants or partner organisations will pay for the outcome.

The appropriate next investment is a narrow paid pilot. A broad content depot, extensive aptitude curriculum, and wider assessment categories should follow only if the central transfer hypothesis survives that pilot.
